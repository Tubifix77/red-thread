Siv stood in the maintenance yard, the notebook in her hand. She had the line number still fresh in her memory. She leaned against the base of the intake housing, looked at the ground. Her fingers curled around the notebook’s spine, the paper thin, the ink faded. She had opened it once before, when the first failure came, and the second. Now it was open again, the pages marked, the margins filled with notes. She didn’t need to read it all, not yet. She had found a way to track the error, and she was going to follow it. The archive was three days away, but she had time. The Provision was running. The people of Hallowmere were waiting. She had her own way of measuring time, her own way of waiting. And the founders had theirs, too. They had written it down, and Siv was going to read it.

The archive was a converted grain store, its walls damp from the seasons, its floors warped by decades of use. Siv had come here once before, when she was younger, and had found nothing but dust and silence. Now she came with purpose, with a notebook, with a line number to trace. The archive had no index, no catalogue, no way of knowing what was where. The founders had left it that way, she suspected, not to make it easier, but to make it harder. The documentation was incomplete on purpose, and she had learned to accept that. She didn’t need to understand it all — only to find what she needed. She moved through the rows of shelves, her fingers brushing the spines of the old manuals. The handwriting was small, the ink faded, the pages brittle. She handled them carefully, the way she handled the Provision’s core, with the same precision and the same fear. She didn’t like the idea of anything being lost, and she didn’t like the idea of anything being left behind. She had spent six years away from Hallowmere, but she had never stopped thinking about it. She had come back to fix what had been broken, and she would not let the founders stop her.

She found the reference in a stack of manuals, the one that had been marked with a faded red stamp. The founders had written it in the margin, in a small, bureaucratic script. She read it once, then again, the words sinking into her mind. The line was marked as an unreachable branch, but the founders had noted it with a figure, a number that had been written in the same margin. She checked it against the current population of Hallowmere, and the arithmetic was clear. The number was not a mistake. It was a choice. The founders had known what they were doing, and they had left the record for someone to find. Siv didn’t feel anything — not yet. She had found what she needed, and she had not said a word about it. She folded the page, slipped it into the notebook, and closed it again. Her hands were steady, her mind quiet. The Provision was running, the people of Hallowmere were waiting. She had her own way of measuring time, her own way of waiting. And she had found what the founders had left behind.
